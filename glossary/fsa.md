title: FSA

A flexible spending account (FSA) lets employees put part of their pay, before taxes, toward eligible expenses. A health FSA covers medical costs like copays and prescriptions; a dependent care FSA covers child or elder care. Unlike an [HSA](/glossary/hsa), an FSA is owned by the employer's plan, and unused money is generally forfeited at the end of the year unless the plan allows a short grace period or a limited carryover.

## Why it matters for a team of 5 to 50

FSAs lower taxable wages, which reduces your [payroll tax](/glossary/payroll-tax) as well as your employees'. With a health FSA, an employee can spend their full annual election early in the year, even before it has all come out of their pay. Elections are set during [open enrollment](/glossary/open-enrollment) and have to flow into payroll as the right [deduction](/glossary/deduction).

Fernly syncs health, dental, vision, 401(k) and FSA deductions from enrollment straight into payroll. [See benefits](/features/benefits).
